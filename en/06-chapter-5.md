**Chapter 5: Strategy recommendations for advancing China's AI open source ecosystem**

**5.1 Development principles and basic strategy**

**Development principles: adhere to long-termism, strengthen patient capital, optimize functional paths, and develop inclusively.**\
Building an AI open source ecosystem should take long-termism as its basic orientation and avoid measuring results by short-term output or interim indicators. It should encourage the growth of "patient capital" able to invest over the long term and support the continuous accumulation of foundational technology, community building and middle-layer capabilities. On development paths, it should suit local conditions based on regional resource endowments and stage of development. At the same time, it should remain open and inclusive, encouraging multiple technology routes and governance models to coexist, achieving ecosystem resilience and diversity through inclusiveness, and avoiding the structural risks of single-path dependence.

**Basic strategy: enterprises and universities as twin drivers, deep integration of the ecosystem with use cases, and development moderately ahead of governance.**

In terms of the driving mechanism, enterprises strengthen engineering capability and industrial conversion while universities consolidate basic research and talent supply, jointly supporting the evolution of the open source ecosystem. On development paths, real industry use cases should lead the way, deeply integrating technology iteration, engineering practice and community reuse so that the ecosystem matures through application. In institutional design, the relationship between development and governance should be positioned according to a region's stage of development and resource endowment: most regions should keep development moderately ahead of governance to maintain market vitality and create stable expectations for multi-stakeholder collaboration, while the few regions with a large AI industry and a deep ecosystem foundation may place governance moderately first.

**5.2 Encourage enterprises to strengthen open source capability building and enhance their role in organizing and driving the ecosystem**

In China's AI industrial structure, leading technology companies have marked advantages in computing power, data, engineering capability and commercial use cases, and are currently the core force best placed to bear the cost of long-term exploration and to guide the evolution of technology routes.

Leading technology companies should be guided to transition from recipients of use cases to ecosystem organizers, contributing general-purpose models, toolchains, framework systems and engineering capabilities through open source to build an open, shared technology foundation; to keep accumulating reusable open source assets and standard interfaces in real application use cases, building long-term, iterative public technical capability and becoming a technology center that is "used, depended on and extended"; to reserve ecosystem space for small and medium-sized enterprises and startup teams through open interfaces, plug-in mechanisms and development toolchains, promoting multi-stakeholder participation and capability diffusion and strengthening the layered structure and job-absorption capacity of the industrial network; and, in policy support and evaluation systems, to fully recognize companies' long-term maintenance responsibilities, community governance contributions and international collaboration efforts in the open source ecosystem, improving stable expectations and sustained incentive mechanisms. This will promote a virtuous cycle of use-case-driven development, ecosystem amplification and industrial expansion.

Leading technology companies are also key vehicles for talent development and an indispensable link in moving open source capability from learning to practice. They should be supported in co-building open source learning and practice centers with open source communities and social organizations, carrying out progressive training around real projects to create a growth path from basic contributions to module maintenance to community governance. Companies should be encouraged to draw on mature open source management practice and bring open source participation into talent development, job-grade promotion and professional evaluation systems, turning open source from an individual activity into an "institutionalized professional asset". Through systematic and transferable talent mechanisms, large companies can both strengthen their own long-term technological competitiveness and continuously supply the Chinese AI open source ecosystem with core talent capable of international collaboration.

**5.3 Push universities to accelerate open source ecosystem development and turn them into AI open source ecosystem hubs**

Universities are an important source of talent and original work and an important driver of the AI open source ecosystem. AI open source projects should be incorporated into university research evaluation and talent development systems, with recognition for non-paper outputs such as code contributions, community influence and the degree of engineering reuse; universities should be supported in building long-term maintenance-oriented open source projects around foundation models, algorithm frameworks, evaluation systems and data tools, creating foundational public infrastructure for the AI industry.

Universities and enterprises should be encouraged to form collaborative mechanisms around open source projects, using university–enterprise, practice-oriented "Learning Centers" as the lever to establish sustainable open source engineering capability development and all-round quality development systems at universities. A Learning Center can be led by a university, with enterprises providing real needs and engineering mentors and jointly building courses and a project pool; using real open source projects for graduation projects or as credit-bearing work lets students develop the ability to "do engineering in a community" while giving enterprises reusable components and incremental talent. Interdisciplinary joint courses and project-based teaching break down the barriers between algorithms, engineering, product and industry knowledge, equipping students with basic use-case abstraction ability and the ability to judge the boundaries of value. At the same time, the development process should strengthen the sense of responsibility and of continuous maintenance, encouraging students to periodically optimize and version-iterate the modules they own so that they understand the engineering standards of long-term availability and sustainable delivery.

With systematic support, universities can provide the talent foundation, with the ability to produce in an integrated way, for distributed innovation and OPC development under the AI open source ecosystem.

**5.4 Give scientific play to the role of local governments: optimize public services, promote industrial collaboration, improve ecosystem development**

Local governments have a distinctive advantage in China's AI ecosystem: they can connect concrete industry use cases and influence resource allocation through policy instruments.

Governments should take part in building the AI open source ecosystem in a rational and restrained way, maintaining a dynamic balance among "delegating power, empowering, providing a backstop and governing". Aiming to supply public goods, build collaboration platforms, hold the line on downside risks and establish transparent rules, they should lay out moderately ahead of time in areas such as intellectual property boundaries, open source compliance frameworks, data use standards, interface standards and evaluation systems, providing stable expectations for multi-stakeholder collaboration and reducing institutional uncertainty. By supplying public goods such as computing platforms, data sandboxes and real pilot use cases, they can lower the cost of exploration for companies and communities and give asset-light innovators such as OPCs the basic conditions to enter and participate sustainably, so that technical capability can be converted directly into service and market capability without depending on an asset-heavy organizational structure as an intermediary.

Real use cases should drive ecosystem evolution: guiding technology to keep iterating through engineering practice; returning interim results to the community in open source form so they become reusable industry modules and tool components; and gradually building a full-chain structure around use cases, from foundation models and the plug-in ecosystem to industry-adaptation modules, third-party toolchains, consulting services and secondary development. This forms a virtuous cycle of "use-case-driven development → engineering iteration → open source reuse → industrial expansion", allowing ecosystem capabilities to keep evolving at key nodes of the industry chain instead of stopping at isolated pilots or short-term projects. In this process, attention should be paid to modularity and the openness of interfaces so that actors of different sizes can all find a place to plug into the ecosystem chain, creating space for the continued participation of OPCs and extending employment from concentrated absorption toward distributed collaboration.

Evaluation criteria should shift from project assessment to ecosystem health assessment. Contribution should not be measured only by company size or revenue, and indicators such as community activity, the number of core contributors, the degree of cross-party collaboration and international influence should be gradually incorporated into the evaluation system, so that the sustained contributions of asset-light actors such as OPCs can be identified and counted, creating positive incentives for long-term engineering investment and professional division of labor. Trial and error should be encouraged, interim failures tolerated and long-term engineering investment supported, creating a cultural environment that respects engineering practice and sustained contribution and preventing short-term KPI orientation from weakening ecosystem resilience.

**5.5 Support social organizations and neutral platforms to close the ecosystem's neutrality gap**

Based on the experience of open source ecosystem development, social organizations and neutral platforms are better placed than company-led or government-driven models to play a coordinating role across companies, regions and interests.

Support should be given to specialized open source foundations and community organizations that operate independently of government administration, and their independent governance status should be made clear; they should be encouraged to play a role in standards discussion, community governance, compliance support and international connections; and institutional design should strengthen their long-term sustainability and prevent project-based, short-term operations.

The growth of neutral social organizations will help raise the level of trust and the density of collaboration in China's AI open source ecosystem, laying the groundwork for governance at a more advanced stage in the future.

**5.6 Let development stay moderately ahead of governance, promote learning across society, and actively cultivate an inclusive engineering culture**

International experience shows that mature open source governance systems are usually built on highly active ecosystems with diverse participants and full competition among technology routes. The AI industry is still at an early stage of ecosystem development, and China's AI open source ecosystem remains seriously lacking in infrastructure, de facto standards and the density of cross-organizational collaboration; the object of governance has not yet fully come into being. Policy and industry therefore need a clear consensus: **development is the first priority, and the core task is to "broaden participation, thicken the technology layer, multiply testbeds and widen application points"; development should stay moderately ahead of governance, and governance should serve development rather than replace it; for open source projects of different models and at different stages, differentiated governance structures should be allowed to coexist, avoiding one-size-fits-all regulatory requirements.**

On that basis, regions that already have a large AI industrial base and a deep pool of AI talent may place governance "moderately first", bounded by not obstructing innovation, focusing on baseline risks (security, compliance, intellectual property, supply chain trustworthiness) and basic rules (license compliance, contributor agreements, vulnerability response), and following a path of "promoting governance through use, with light governance and strong services". A reusable open source technology stack, toolchain and industry practice should come first, with operable norms and standards accumulating through practice afterwards. Indicators for evaluating the ecosystem should emphasize "effective supply" rather than "complete institutions".

International experience shows that a mature open source ecosystem requires an ample supply of talent and the formation of an inclusive engineering culture. The AI era is accelerating the movement of engineering and other professional capabilities out of universities and companies and into the wider public. Open practice communities and public collaboration spaces should be encouraged, and open source challenges, public data proving grounds and industry-adaptation co-creation activities should be opened to society, embedding the learning process directly in real application use cases. Institutionally, China should foster a social practice environment of "learning while using and promoting learning through use", so that technology enthusiasts, industry practitioners and people from other fields can all take part in ecosystem building by contributing plug-ins, optimizing processes or improving model applications. By increasing the density and frequency of social participation, an engineering culture that respects collaboration, recognizes contributions and embraces multiple paths of exploration will gradually take shape. At China's current stage, policy and governance should serve the incubation of that culture and those mechanisms rather than converging the space for exploration prematurely in pursuit of complete governance.
