**Chapter 2: Why China must accelerate the development of the AI open source ecosystem**

President Xi Jinping noted: "Accelerating the development of the new generation of artificial intelligence is an important strategic lever for winning the initiative in global technological competition, and an important strategic resource for promoting leapfrog development of China's science and technology, upgrading its industries and raising its overall productivity."\[7\]. This confirms that **from the outset, China's AI open source ecosystem has pursued development goals quite different from those of other countries: open source sharing, technology empowerment, innovative employment, and self-reliance and controllability.**

**2.1 National strategic significance: staying ahead of the times, strengthening national competitiveness**

In the *Opinions on Deeply Implementing the "AI Plus" Initiative*, released by the State Council in August 2025, the government made clear that the open source ecosystem should be made to flourish and that multidimensional coordination mechanisms should be built\[8\]. In January 2026, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) and seven other departments issued the *Implementation Opinions on the Special Action for "AI Plus Manufacturing"*, setting the goal of "building a world-leading open source and open ecosystem" by 2027 and setting out paths including building open source communities, deploying open source projects and making open source culture flourish\[9\].

Today, in key links such as large models, foundational algorithms and computing platforms, the global AI industry is highly concentrated in a handful of multinational technology giants, forming a new "technology monopoly structure". This structure is visible not only in market share but also in deeper strategic powers: the right to set standards, the right to choose the direction of technological evolution, and the right to define ecosystem rules. Crucially, because the AI ecosystem is public technology infrastructure, its construction and the competition around it are subject to a pronounced time window. Once developers, enterprises and public-sector bodies become path-dependent and "locked in" to a particular technology ecosystem, the cost of substituting technology for latecomer countries rises markedly. Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem is a typical example. Developing an AI open source ecosystem is therefore an important institutional instrument for China to re-establish technological sovereignty in the new round of technological competition. It can help break down closed ecosystem moats through open collaboration, reduce systemic dependence on a single technology supply system, and provide an important route to the AI development strategy goal set by the State Council — that "major breakthroughs be made in the basic theory of artificial intelligence, and that its technology and applications partly reach a world-leading level**"**\[10\]. It is a realistic channel for China's transition from technology chaser to co-builder of rules.

**2.2 Local development significance: empowering industrial upgrading, advancing local development**

The 2026 Government Work Report called for "accelerating the promotion of a new generation of smart terminals and agents, advancing the commercial and large-scale application of artificial intelligence in key industries, and cultivating new forms of AI-native business and new models", and for "supporting the development of AI open source communities and promoting the prosperity of the open source ecosystem"\[12\]. The intelligent economy is, in essence, a reshaping of productivity and a reconstruction of business logic. At present, as "AI Plus" helps local industries upgrade, three main bottlenecks stand out. First, **acquisition costs are high**: scarce AI talent, tight computing power and fragmented vertical-industry data together make any single AI+ project a heavy investment\[13\]. Second, **applications lack breadth and depth**: individual AI+ application projects tend to concentrate on a single use case, and underlying technical capabilities are hard to retain locally as general-purpose tools and talent capabilities that can evolve sustainably; it is hard to drive deeper expansion of technical capability or to form compoundable technical assets in time, which limits the development of long-term innovation capacity. Third, **AI talent supply and demand are out of balance**: AI is not deeply enough integrated with local industries, and people with cross-disciplinary literacy and practical ability remain scarce, which can leave local AI development in a passive position of high input, low accumulation and weak autonomy. Against this backdrop, a sustainable AI open source ecosystem levels the playing field at the foundation layer and builds a large "co-created think tank"; it is a key lever for breaking out of the current predicament of AI transformation in traditional local industries and for building a local system of long-term innovation capacity\[14\]. It is characterized by collaborative innovation, deep integration and continuous evolution, and it accelerates innovation and empowers local industrial development through cost reduction, talent development and use-case-driven approaches.

**First, the AI open source ecosystem is a new engine for lowering research and development costs**. Building on open source large models (such as DeepSeek and Qwen) and the ecosystems derived from them, local enterprises do not have to bear the full cost of pretraining; they need only fine-tune with local industry data and adapt to their use cases, markedly lowering the threshold for computing power and R&D investment. More importantly, the AI open source ecosystem turns a one-off purchase into continuous iteration. Local technical capabilities can absorb external improvements in a shared environment, achieving capability upgrades at low cost. This mechanism moves localities from "high-cost, import-based development" to "co-evolutionary development", creating a new institutional basis for long-term technological accumulation.

**Second, the AI open source ecosystem is a booster for developing technical talent**. It offers a talent development model "with projects as the vehicle and communities as the bond". By building open source communities that bring together universities, enterprises and developers and treating real local industry use cases as co-creation targets, it can achieve a threefold transformation: teaching becomes use-case-based, enterprises become collaborative, and talent becomes visible. University courses are built around real open source projects, so students acquire engineering skills by taking part in practice; enterprises post tasks and module-optimization work through open source projects and build a talent pool; and developers' contribution records are public and transparent, forming quantifiable proof of technical ability and strengthening career mobility and growth paths. This mechanism shifts talent development from degree-based certification to contribution-based certification, and from short-term job matching to long-term capability evolution. Once a locality has a stable open source collaboration network, technical capability no longer depends on individual mobility but can accumulate sustainably within the region, easing the structural risk created by the outflow of top talent.

**Finally, the AI open source ecosystem is an amplifier of industry use-case resources**. Through co-building and sharing, it turns industry use-case resources from closed assets into vehicles for collaborative innovation. By opening up industry data standards and interface specifications, different enterprises can share non-sensitive data and model outputs within a unified technical framework; by jointly releasing open source projects, typical use cases can be abstracted into reusable modules that travel across industries. When multiple industry use cases connect to the same open source technology foundation, the results of innovation compound: improvements in one use case feed back into others, producing networked diffusion. This mechanism breaks through the limits of "isolated demonstration projects" and moves local innovation from isolated experiments to systematic collaboration.

**2.3 Market development significance: spawning business models, boosting innovative employment**

Open source lowers the barrier to technology adoption and is an excellent way to acquire and attract users; it can achieve product-market fit (PMF) quickly. In the software era it was an important incubator of business models, giving rise to models such as paid SaaS, paid professional services (as with Red Hat), advertising revenue and ecosystem revenue (as with Android)\[16\]. Unlike the software and internet industries, the AI industry is inherently characterized by high dynamic inference costs, weak economies of scale and heavy ecosystem dependence, and its business models are evolving from a "product-centric" paradigm to one driven by both services and the ecosystem\[4\]. In just a few years, entirely new business models have emerged.

Open source plays the role of an accelerator in the evolution of AI business models. By lowering the R&D threshold, shortening the market cold-start cycle, reducing the trust cost for industry customers and building technical standards and path dependence, it markedly compresses the time cost of technology diffusion and commercial validation. Across these AI business models, open source is no longer merely a philanthropic form of technology sharing but an important strategic tool with which companies compete for ecosystem niches and shape market structure.

Moreover, the AI era is an era of intelligence plug-ins and skill plug-ins. A "super individual economy" is rising, and a new form of One-Person Company (OPC) built around "one person + AI" is reshaping the boundaries of traditional industries with unprecedented agility, creativity and cost efficiency\[18\]. The skill threshold has fallen sharply, individuals develop from specialists into "full-stack" operators, and efficiency improves from linear to exponential. Organizational forms are being restructured, shifting from "people managing people" to "people managing AI"\[19\]. Individual ventures now compete on "use-case insight" and "industry understanding", the cost of trial and error has fallen markedly, and venture cycles have shortened dramatically; innovative employment has become an important source of job growth. From the central government down to local governments, policies aimed at cultivating the OPC ecosystem have been issued in quick succession, and competition and cooperation among cities over the "super individual" — the core innovation unit of future industries — has begun. The AI open source ecosystem is undoubtedly the public technology foundation and collaboration network of the OPC ecosystem. Building open source communities and opening up data and computing infrastructure can markedly lower the barrier to individual innovation and spawn derivative businesses around open source projects, such as services, custom development and systems integration. Job growth can then take the form of "ecosystem-wide diffusion" rather than "project-based fluctuation", strengthening the resilience and counter-cyclical capacity of regional economies and employment.

**2.4 Talent development significance: bridging industry and academia, accelerating talent growth**

The AI open source ecosystem is important infrastructure for AI talent development and capability evolution. Compared with traditional closed systems of education and R&D, it provides a public platform on which people of different levels and backgrounds can continuously take part in technological evolution and industry practice, effectively easing the current structural mismatch between AI talent supply and industry demand.

As the AI industry advances rapidly, talent has become an increasingly important bottleneck constraining the scale and depth of industrial development. On the one hand, AI technology is complex and iterates quickly, and relying solely on university education or in-house corporate training can hardly keep covering the latest technological paradigms and engineering practice. On the other hand, in closed source environments core technologies and real business use cases are highly closed to outsiders, so many potential talents have no access to high-quality code, real engineering problems or industry-grade collaboration processes. The result is a structural talent gap: people "know theory but lack practice", or "understand models but not applications".

By opening up code, documentation and the collaboration process, the open source ecosystem turns the evolution of AI technology from an internal activity of a few organizations into a public process that can be widely observed, joined and learned from. Developers can not only work directly with cutting-edge models, tools and application frameworks, but also take part in decomposing problems, discussing solutions and co-building code in real projects, gradually accumulating engineering capability and systems thinking. At the same time, the contribution mechanisms of open source communities give talent a low-threshold, quantifiable and verifiable channel to demonstrate ability, shifting the assessment of technical ability from degrees and backgrounds to actual contributions, which helps talent grow faster and improves the efficiency with which talent and industry demand are matched.

In their 2026 *Implementation Plan for the Special Action on "AI Plus Manufacturing"*, MIIT and seven other departments proposed "building high-level AI open source communities … forecasting demand for AI industry talent … supporting universities and enterprises … [to] develop multidisciplinary talent"\[9\]. By placing open source communities, industry demand and talent development within a single action framework, it stresses that the open source ecosystem is an important vehicle for producing "multidisciplinary talent with both industry awareness and hands-on technical skills". In December 2025, the Ministry of Education updated its *Catalog of Vocational Education Majors*, adding the majors "Open Source Technology Application" and "Open Source Technology Development and Application". The OpenAtom Foundation has taken this as an opportunity to work with ecosystem partners along a chain that runs from jointly building the curriculum, to improving faculty capabilities, to real project practice, to contribution-based evaluation and talent certification, to internships and employment, noting that mechanisms such as open source project practice and the accumulation of contribution evaluation data can produce high-caliber open source technical talent who "can get hands-on, collaborate and deliver"\[20\]. "Taking part in the open source ecosystem" has become an explicit path in talent development and a basis for evaluation.
