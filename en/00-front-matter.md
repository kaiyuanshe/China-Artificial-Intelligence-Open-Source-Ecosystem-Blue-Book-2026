Preface**

In the latest wave of artificial intelligence, represented by large models and agents, technology is reshaping production methods and social structures at unprecedented speed. Open source, the most vital collaborative innovation mechanism of the digital-intelligence era, is leaping from a software development paradigm to the infrastructure and innovation network of the AI wave, and has become a key force in lowering technical barriers, accelerating the diffusion of innovation, reshaping the industrial landscape and boosting innovative employment. China attaches great importance to building the AI open source ecosystem. This year's Government Work Report stressed the need to "create new forms of the intelligent economy" and explicitly called for "supporting the development of AI open source communities and promoting the prosperity of the open source ecosystem." Against this background, this blue paper systematically explains the meaning and structure of the AI open source ecosystem, and sets out that it is not merely a technical issue but a comprehensive proposition that bears on national competitiveness, the path of industrial upgrading and the talent development system.

Unlike the largely market-driven open source ecosystems of the United States and Europe, China's AI open source ecosystem has developed distinctly Chinese characteristics of "application-driven + multi-stakeholder collaboration": leading companies build ecosystem entry points through open source projects and related activities, local governments promote ecosystem cultivation through institutional provision and resource integration, and universities and communities play an important role in supplying talent and accumulating capabilities. This distinctive path of multi-stakeholder collaboration gives China the potential to move "from application prosperity to ecosystem building" within a short period, but it also brings challenges in understanding and in institutional design.

At present, China's AI open source ecosystem still faces several critical bottlenecks. First, understanding of an "open source ecosystem" remains at the level of a "collection of projects", lacking systematic collaborative thinking. Second, the links between technology, industry and talent are weak, making it difficult to accumulate capabilities sustainably. Third, the conversion of innovation into value is insufficient, and job creation is limited. Fourth, neutral governance and public infrastructure are relatively weak, and ecosystem resilience needs to improve. Fifth, in the global technology system, self-reliance and controllability need to be strengthened. The essence of these problems is that the shift in understanding from "project thinking" to "ecosystem thinking" has not yet been made.

On this basis, the blue paper is organized around five core questions: what it is, why it matters, what the current situation is, what the problems are, and how to advance it. It first defines the key elements of the AI open source ecosystem and how it operates; it then makes the case for its strategic significance at the national, local, market and talent levels; it goes on to review the current state of China's AI open source ecosystem and its typical models, and reveals structural differences through a China–US comparison; on that basis it analyzes the core problems in a systematic way; and finally it proposes collaborative development paths and strategy recommendations for government, enterprises, universities and social organizations.

We hope this blue paper will not only offer a panoramic view of China's AI open source ecosystem but, more importantly, provide decision-makers and practitioners with a methodological reference for moving from "isolated breakthroughs" to "systematic construction", and help China move as quickly as possible from participant to ecosystem builder and co-builder of rules in the global AI competition.
