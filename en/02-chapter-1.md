**Chapter 1: What is an AI open source ecosystem**

1.1 Open source and the open source ecosystem

**Open source** originated in "open source software" and was formally introduced by the Open Source Initiative (OSI) in 1998. It refers to software whose source code is open and which is released under an open source license, so that it may be freely published, copied, modified and used. Today, open source is viewed more broadly as a new development model in which large groups of people rely on internet platforms, participating and collaborating to continuously accumulate collective intelligence and achieve sustained innovation\[1\]**.** It has become one of the important pieces of public infrastructure of the digital economy, and the wellspring of innovation, openness, sharing and sustainable development in that economy. After decades of development, open source has become the mainstream development model of the modern software industry. At present, 96% of commercial codebases worldwide contain open source software, and 77% of the code in commercial codebases comes directly from open source software\[2\].

An **open source ecosystem** is a technology innovation ecosystem built around open source projects that brings together resources from many parties. It is a dynamic system made up of many participants — developers, contributors, projects, communities, enterprises, educational institutions and policymakers — that aims to advance software innovation and technological progress through open source code and collaborative development. In its 2021 *Open Source Ecosystem White Paper*, the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology (CAICT) defined the concept as follows: "The open source ecosystem is built around open source projects and comprises five kinds of industry-chain elements: open source communities made up of contributors, open source contributors in various industries, and users in various industries. Open source business models, open source community operations and open source risk governance are the three links that ecosystem development focuses on"\[3\]. It follows that the open source ecosystem is the soil and rainforest in which open source projects thrive, and the fundamental guarantee that the idea of open source keeps developing, creating value and producing broad social and economic impact.

**1.2 AI open source and the AI open source ecosystem**

**AI open source**, like traditional software open source, is an open, shared and distributed model of collaborative development, but the object of openness extends from code repositories and development tools in the traditional sense to diverse technology assets such as AI-based models, corpora and datasets, and training frameworks\[4\]. In 2024, the Open Source Initiative (OSI) formally released the *Open Source AI Definition 1.0*. That definition carries forward the basic spirit of open source software, requires open models to be open and reproducible in key components such as weights, code and training data, and sets out core principles including the freedom to study, the freedom to modify and the freedom to distribute, establishing a basic framework for AI open source\[5\]. In practice, the degree of openness varies considerably between models: according to the tiered classification of model openness published by the Center for Research on Foundation Models at Stanford University, there are several gradual levels between closed source and fully open\[6\].

An **AI open source ecosystem**, and especially a large model ecosystem, is a technology innovation ecosystem built around AI open source projects that brings together resources from many parties. Compared with a traditional software open source ecosystem, it has markedly stronger network effects and much greater room for empowerment.

In terms of network effects, a traditional software ecosystem usually revolves around a single project and iterates linearly on an existing code base, whereas an AI open source ecosystem spans multiple layers — model architectures, training data, inference frameworks, application tools and more — and multiple routes such as large language models, multimodal models and specialized small models can be advanced and interwoven at the same time by different actors in the ecosystem. This multi-element collaborative production environment markedly increases the complexity of the ecosystem and produces more flexible forms of collaboration between actors, forming a dynamic, complex and highly interconnected ecosystem network.

In terms of empowerment, the value of traditional software open source lies mainly in marginal efficiency gains, whereas large models and agent systems, through an "intelligence plug-in" mechanism, directly compress intermediate forms of labor and overturn the structure of value distribution in many industries. AI also reshapes the way people work, moving from "people using tools" to "people setting goals, with AI breaking them down and executing them". The barrier to starting a business has fallen markedly, and "one-person companies" (OPCs — One-Person Company, a new form of venture in which a single person uses AI tools to run a complete business loop) have emerged on an unprecedented scale. The room it creates for empowerment has multiple dimensions: horizontal expansion across industries, vertical restructuring within industries, and the extension of empowerment from enterprises to individuals.
